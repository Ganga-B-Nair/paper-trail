One real finding is doing the work of three claims it does not support.

Mixed
1 checkable 2 unverifiable as written 2 rhetoric, not evidence

A new study proves that students who use AI tools score 40% higher on exams, which is why every serious university has already made them mandatory. Critics who disagree simply haven't read the research.

  • 01

    A new study proves that students who use AI tools score 40% higher on exams

    Unverifiable as writtenLoad-bearing

    No study is named, so the figure cannot be traced. “Proves” also overstates what a single study can do — and a score gap does not establish that the tools caused it.

    What would settle itThe citation: which study, what sample size, was it randomised, and 40% higher than what baseline.

  • 02

    every serious university has already made them mandatory

    False as stated

    A universal claim that one counterexample defeats, and “serious” is doing quiet work — it lets any university that hasn't be dismissed as not serious.

    What would settle itA single named university with no such mandate.

  • 03

    Critics who disagree simply haven't read the research.

    Rhetoric, not evidence

    This is not a claim about the world; it is a pre-emptive dismissal that makes disagreement itself the proof of ignorance. Nothing here can be true or false.

    What would settle itNothing — it makes no factual assertion to test.

Rhetorical moves

  • Causal slide — which is why turns an unestablished correlation into a stated reason, without argument.
  • No-true-Scotsman — every serious university makes the claim unfalsifiable by redefining exceptions out of the set.

To check this yourself

  • Ask for the study by name, then read its abstract rather than the summary of it.
  • Check whether the 40% is a test-score difference or a relative change in something else.
  • Look for one named university that has not made the tools mandatory.
